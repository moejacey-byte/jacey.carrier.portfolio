01 / The book

A stepping stone toward a flourishing life

Turn reflection into action.

MÔË Steps to Success is designed as a stepping stone for improving emotional intelligence and personal lifestyle while creating new habits that can support a prosperous life. The book will combine guidance, resources, reflection, goal-setting, and practical opportunities for readers to actively work toward the lives they want.

The project is intentionally personal and global. I plan to document my own travel, service, learning, and growth while collecting observations and research on how people and communities approach education, self-improvement, and opportunity.

My aim is not to prescribe one perfect path. Instead, the project intends to provide broad steps that can be adapted to the individual and used to develop self-improvement and existential skills.

02 / Problem being explored

A question behind the project

“If you could change anything about the education system or yourself, what would it be, and why? What’s stopping you or us as a community from committing to improvement and change?”

Re-imagining the self and education system as a united front.

I see a genuine need to better prepare young people for the requirements of modern society. My research begins from the concern that students can leave school without enough preparation for the emotional, professional, personal, and practical demands of adulthood.

I believe education can move beyond a narrow focus on grades by helping students understand themselves, recognize their strengths, navigate emotions, build motivation, and learn how to find resources and solve problems independently.

The theory behind the project is that if self-awareness and proactive mindsets become standard parts of learning, students can develop habits that support them not only as students, but as adults who continue learning throughout life.

03 / Research methods & philosophy

Learning through travel, service, observation & reflection

Research that moves with me.

The project combines personal reflection with community service, travel, observation, conversations, and research into schools and education models around the world. Volunteering in schools, orphanages, elephant sanctuaries, sustainable-living and education centers, and other community settings is part of the project's learning journey.

Travel is also a research tool: exploring different societies broadens my understanding of humans and social life while helping me identify resources, jobs, grants, internships, education programs, and other opportunities that can be made more accessible to young people.

Intellectual influences named in the project include Jim Kwik, Robert Greene, Albert Einstein, Howard Gardner, and Sir Ken Robinson. Aristotle is also central to the project's framing of flourishing, purpose, and reflection.

04 / Re-imagining the education system

Four components for the classroom

A more holistic model for learning and life.

As young people move into adulthood, responsibilities can make it difficult to preserve time for self-discovery, experimentation, innovation, and ongoing learning. This curriculum research asks how schools can make those capacities part of normal education rather than something students must discover later on their own.

The model brings four components together so learners can connect what happens in the classroom with how they actually navigate life.

01

Universal Design for Learning

Use multiple pathways for learning, recognize individual preferences and comfort zones, and create inclusive spaces where different types of learners can effectively participate.

02

Social-emotional learning

Develop self- and social awareness, understand and manage emotions, set and achieve positive goals, practice empathy, build relationships, and make responsible decisions.

03

Existential skills

Apply classroom and book learning to real life, use available resources, develop an individual sense of meaning, exercise agency, and create a personal view of what makes life feel whole.

04

Executive functions

Understand how the brain supports everyday tasks, recognize strengths and weaknesses, and develop practical strategies for planning, focus, decision-making, organization, and self-management.

06 / Why I am fit to do this

Lead by example

I want to turn lived experience into useful education.

I believe I need to lead by example and take action within this project. My background in education, youth development, teaching, and international learning gives me a foundation for studying the needs of students and translating what I learn into practice.

My experiences in Upward Bound and AmeriCorps gave me opportunities to work with high school students from different backgrounds and to teach areas including English Literature, Financial Literacy, and College Ready Skills. Those experiences helped me see the need for education that recognizes the whole student.

I bring resilience, adaptability, and a self-starter mindset to this work. The project began as a personally funded effort to travel, learn, volunteer, and write. I intend to continue expanding that work through research, community engagement, curriculum development, and eventually MÔË Academy for Leaders.

The long-term vision

A book that becomes a curriculum — and a curriculum that becomes a school.

The research behind MÔË Steps to Success is intended to keep evolving through teaching, travel, reflection, community service, and collaboration. The future goal is to bring these ideas into MÔË Academy for Leaders.
